## Summary

Ryan Blosser and Trevor Piersall, co-founders of Shenandoah Permaculture Institute, bring a grounded and evolving vision of what permaculture means for food systems and community education. Rather than settling for herb spirals and aesthetic gardens, they articulate permaculture as a rigorous design process rooted in three ethical pillars—earth care, people care, and fair share—that generates regenerative systems adapted to place. What strikes listeners is their pragmatism: permaculture isn't ideology, but a tool. In their classroom, ideological diversity becomes a feature, not a bug. A retired road-layer and a 22-year-old trust-fund pagan find common ground through the work itself, discovering that when people show up to learn, empathy can bridge what seemed unbridgeable. Their book is full of these stories—moments where the work of growing things and thinking carefully about land reveals our shared humanity.

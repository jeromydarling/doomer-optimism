## Summary

Alicia Pedersen, a PhD in Renaissance literature turned urban advocate and developer, discusses courtyard urbanism—a building typology common in European cities for centuries but virtually absent from American development for over a hundred years. Drawing on her experience raising three children in Chicago while pursuing neighborhood advocacy, Pedersen explains how courtyard buildings—mid-rise structures (4–5 stories) organized around shared interior courtyards—offer a humane alternative to both sprawling suburbs and isolated high-rises. These buildings naturally support mixed-income families, intergenerational community, local retail, and aging in place, while remaining affordable to construct when freed from restrictive setback requirements and unnecessary building codes. Pedersen has recently incorporated Courtyard Urbanist and is collaborating with developers, architects, and innovators (including Jan Sramac's California Forever project) to translate this historical form into buildable, code-compliant designs for modern American markets. She frames this moment as analogous to the Renaissance—a period of civilizational renewal following disruption—where regulatory reform, technological innovation in construction, and widespread desire for walkable urban life converge to make beautiful, sustainable cities possible.

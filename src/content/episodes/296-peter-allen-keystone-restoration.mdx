## Summary

Elizabeth Oldfield traces her vocational journey from BBC media producer to think tank director to podcaster and community-builder, reflecting on how to create meaningful change in fractured times. She argues that real transformation happens not through top-down policy work, but through the painstaking cultural and relational work of helping different tribes understand and value each other. Drawing on neurobiology, theology, and lived experience in an intentional community, Oldfield articulates a theory of change rooted in listening, hospitality, and the renewal of institutions—not their destruction. She explores how practices like deep listening, embodied gathering, and commitment to local community become acts of resistance against algorithmic isolation and polarization. For Ashley and the Doomer Optimism network, she offers both philosophical grounding and practical wisdom: the unglamorous work of showing up, asking for help, and building the social technologies (church, school boards, dinner tables) that have sustained human flourishing for generations.

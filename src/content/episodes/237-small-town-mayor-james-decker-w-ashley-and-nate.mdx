## Summary

James Decker, mayor of Stamford, Texas (pop. 3,000), articulates a pragmatic localism rooted in place commitment rather than partisan allegiance. He navigates the paradox of rural sustainability in a globalized age: the necessity of embracing tools like broadband and remote work to arrest population decline, balanced against the threat of cultural homogenization that destroys what made a place worth staying in. His vision rejects both naive nostalgia and technocratic solutionism, insisting instead on the piecemeal, unglamorous work of rebuilding local economies, schools, and institutions as interconnected wholes. The conversation moves from civic non-partisanship through agricultural policy to mental health—each revealing how fragmentation at the systemic level cascades into human despair. Decker's work prefigures a necessary scaling-down, an acceptance that some problems yield only to community-level curation and the slow accumulation of small, rooted commitments.

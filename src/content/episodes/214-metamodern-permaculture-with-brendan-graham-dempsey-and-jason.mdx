## Summary

This episode explores metamodernism as a practical framework for addressing ecological and cultural crisis—one that moves beyond the paralysis of postmodern critique or the naive optimism of technological solutionism. Brendan Graham Dempsey maps how metamodernism integrates premodern wisdom (stewardship, piety), modern rationality and efficiency, and postmodern critique of extractivism into a new synthesis centered on "bio-complexity" and human flourishing. Rather than viewing humans as a cancer on nature, metamodern permaculture positions us as potential "shepherds of bio-complexity"—capable of using our unique rational consciousness to facilitate ecosystemic depth and resilience. The conversation weaves through complexity theory, spiritual practice, political economy, and the implementation of these ideas at Sky Meadow Institute in Vermont, emphasizing that meaningful systems change requires simultaneous work at cultural, institutional, and individual levels without waiting for a perfect solution.

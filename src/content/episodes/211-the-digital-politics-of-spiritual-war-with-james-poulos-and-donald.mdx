## Summary

James Poulos joins us to examine the spiritual crisis unfolding beneath the digital age—not as distant apocalypse, but as present catastrophe already internalized. Drawing on McLuhan's theory of media, Tocqueville's analysis of democratic atomization, and a rich Catholic intellectual tradition, Poulos argues that we have migrated from the televisual age (imagination-driven) to the digital age (memory-driven), a shift with profound consequences for the human soul. The technological dream of world unity through unprecedented connectivity turns out to be continuous with older utopian fantasies—the atomic bomb, aerial bombardment—all motivated by what he calls "the desire to recreate paradise in a place hidden from God." Yet Poulos refuses doom. Instead, he insists on spiritual discipline first—the purification of the heart through Orthodox Christian practice—and then the strategic deployment of tools like Bitcoin, not as escape but as defensive infrastructure for those committed to building alternative markets and communities beyond the reach of algorithmic control.

For listeners seeking concrete steps: abandon smartphones, leave social media platforms, practice the sacraments and family life, grow food, engage in physical labor. But also, he suggests, don't retreat entirely. Learn Bitcoin. Participate in distributed networks. Use technology defensively where necessary, but only after rooting yourself in prayer, in the wisdom of the desert fathers, in a God who loves you with an intimacy no algorithm can simulate.

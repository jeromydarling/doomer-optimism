## Summary

This episode explores the infrastructure and philosophy of food hubs through a conversation between Jason (host) and Drew Zucchino, executive director of Tractor Farms Food Hub in Spruce Pine, North Carolina. Drew traces his path from conventional blueberry farming—where local produce sat unsold while imported fruit occupied supermarket shelves—to building a system that aggregates small regional farms and connects them directly to local consumers, SNAP clients, clinics, and food pantries. Food hubs, he argues, are fundamentally about paying farmers more and increasing access to quality food. Yet the deepest challenge isn't logistical; it's cultural and economic. Young people can't afford land. Investors demand quantifiable returns on systems that derive their real value from intangibles: soil health, community resilience, cultural transmission, the unmeasurable benefit of a child eating a perfect blueberry for the first time.

Drew insists that local food systems work not despite their messiness and resistance to standardization, but because of it. The vibes—connection, trust, quality—matter more than metrics. He's learned that the real work happens across a decade or more, that you cannot have it all in farming, and that the most honest path forward is to build platforms for the next generation rather than promise yourself financial security. Both he and Jason have come to see their work as an offering to a future they won't inhabit, and in that surrender lies unexpected freedom.

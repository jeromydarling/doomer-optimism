## Summary

This episode unspools the thesis of John Williams' influential book on Zen Buddhism and technology in the West: that from the 1890s onward, Western intellectuals—alarmed by industrial mechanization—projected spiritual alternatives onto Asia, discovering in Buddhism a fantasy of machines and wisdom living in harmony. Yet this Orientalist recovery, now crystallized in Silicon Valley's meditation-hacking, deflects us from harder truths. Williams argues we are, and always have been, technological beings; the problem is not technology itself but who controls the infrastructures that now shape our thinking. The conversation pivots from academic analysis to lived practice: How do we resist disenchantment without retreating into caves? How do we organize collectively when mass-media politics weaponizes our divisions? The answer, tentatively, is neither romantic primitivism nor hyper-technologization, but the hard work of reclaiming our own traditions—Christian, agrarian, democratic—to imagine technologies that help us flourish rather than diminish.

At stake is whether we believe in relational being, in the capacity to think *with* rather than *against* the world. Or whether we've surrendered that belief to the machine.

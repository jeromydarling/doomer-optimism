## Summary

This episode inaugurates "Beef Week" with Mike Callicrate, who details the systematic consolidation of the beef industry from a once-competitive market with twenty regional packers to a cartelized system dominated by four corporations. Callicrate describes his experience as a feedlot operator watching IBP, Cargill, and National Beef methodically eliminate competition through territorial delegation and predatory bidding—collapsing the share of consumer dollars reaching ranchers from 82% in the 1970s to as low as 27% during COVID. The conversation situates this industrial capture within a larger ecology of failure: depleted aquifers and topsoil, a fragile food system held hostage by multinational corporations indifferent to domestic food security, and regulatory capture that renders the 1921 Packers and Stockyards Act effectively unenforceable.

What emerges is a portrait of how American agriculture became a machine for extracting wealth from rural communities while externalizing costs onto land, water, workers, and animals. Yet within this grim accounting lies a practical thesis: the way out begins not with revolution but with honoring the proven systems that built dignified rural life—competitive markets, maker-owned enterprises, regenerative land stewardship, and the restoration of antitrust enforcement. Callicrate's work through Ranch Foods Direct and his advocacy exemplify what careful, legal resistance to monopoly power looks like in practice.

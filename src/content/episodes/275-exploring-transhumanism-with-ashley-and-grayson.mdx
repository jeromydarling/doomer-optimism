## Summary

Grayson Quay joins Ashley to discuss his book *The Transhumanist Temptation*, examining how transhumanism represents not a recent phenomenon born from AI, but a centuries-long rejection of natural law rooted in gnosticism, romanticism, liberalism, and capitalism. The conversation traces transhumanism's ideological lineage through poiesis (self-creation) versus mimesis (conformity to transcendent order), arguing that both Marxism and capitalism carry transhumanizing impulses. From artificial wombs to AI companions mimicking the dead, Quay contends that without a coherent philosophy of human flourishing—grounded in either Aristotelian teleology or Christian anthropology—we lack the guardrails to use transformative technologies responsibly. The book's core claim: transhumanism is not anti-technology but anti-limit, anti-nature, and fundamentally hostile to embodied human life as the site of authentic dignity.

What emerges is a sober diagnosis of how individual atomization, corporate incentive structures, and libertarian ideology leave ordinary people defenseless against technologies designed to addict, commodify, and ultimately remake them. Yet hope lies in intermediate institutions—churches, communities, intentional societies—that can collectively resist through shared standards of human good, much as the Amish evaluate technologies by their service to community and God.

## Summary

Ashley Colby Fitzgerald and Leah Sargeant examine two interlocking ideas: first, that women's dignity does not require us to be interchangeable with men, and second, that human flourishing itself depends on acknowledging our fundamental interdependence—not just women's, but everyone's. The book traces how the myth of the autonomous individual has led to contempt for the vulnerable work of care, particularly motherhood, even when wrapped in feminist language. From hormone-suppressing pills to the pressure to "pass as men" in professional life, we see how denying our embodied differences becomes its own form of oppression. The conversation anchors hope not in individual achievement but in the recovery of community, of radial networks of mutual aid, and in the liturgical rhythms that remind us something deeper than productivity shapes a meaningful life.

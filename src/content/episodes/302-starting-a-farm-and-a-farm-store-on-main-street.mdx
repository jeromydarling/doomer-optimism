## Summary

Jason Snyder sits down with Geoffrey Long to discuss his journey from corporate finance to building a regenerative farm and launching a local food store on Main Street. After three years in Chile managing a financial shared service center, Geoffrey returned home to develop an integrated food enterprise: a three-acre food forest with Jersey cows, heritage pigs, pastured poultry, and over forty edible tree species. Balancing a demanding full-time job with serious farming ambitions, he relies on his two oldest children to manage daily operations while plotting an ambitious transition to full-time farming at retirement. The centerpiece of this vision is a downtown storefront combining a zero-waste refillery with a local food grocery—a project nearly three years in renovation that promises to anchor both his farm's market access and his small town's economic revival.

What emerges is a portrait of disciplined pragmatism: Geoffrey acknowledges the time-money tradeoff, the inevitability of compromise (his food forest sits half a mile from the house, not zone-one), and the reality that 100% self-sufficiency is a fallacy. Yet he persists—methodically building infrastructure, learning skills he didn't know he had, and refusing to let failure stop him. His commitment to his kids' understanding of food sovereignty, ecological stewardship, and the work required to build something durable speaks to a deeper belief: that we can act locally with integrity even as large systems fail.

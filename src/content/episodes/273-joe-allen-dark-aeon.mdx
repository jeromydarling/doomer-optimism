## Summary

Recorded on election night 2024, this panel conversation between James Pogue, Jason Snyder, and Ashley Colby Fitzgerald explores the evolving nature and future direction of Doomer Optimism—the loosely-held "structure of feeling" that has grown from a Twitter corner into a network of events, podcast conversations, and on-the-ground projects. The three collaborators grapple with fundamental questions about what DO actually is: explicitly rejecting the "movement" framing in favor of a decentralized, values-based network model rooted in material place-based work. They discuss the tension between maintaining independence and potentially seeking funding; the risk of ideological capture and the need to avoid purity policing; and the importance of supporting hyper-local coalitions (like chicken keepers fighting city bans or farmers seeking land access) rather than imposing top-down ideology. A key insight emerges around local politics as an underexplored frontier—engaging county commissioners and town mayors who operate with practical, bipartisan pragmatism. The panel emphasizes fraternal, voluntary engagement over militant activism, model themselves after civic organizations like the Elks rather than political movements, and stress the need to move beyond Twitter-centricity to include people unable or unwilling to engage in online conflict. They conclude that DO's strength lies in its genuine heterodoxy, its refusal of labels, and its ability to connect isolated, talented people doing regenerative work in their regions.

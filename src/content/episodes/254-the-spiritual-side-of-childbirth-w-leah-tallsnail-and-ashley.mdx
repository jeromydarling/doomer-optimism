## Summary

Birth is not a medical event to be managed or a spiritual awakening to be romanticized—it's a profound encounter with the limits and capacities of the human body, one that reveals depths of yourself you didn't know existed. In this conversation with Leah, a software engineer turned mother, we move past the competing myths of childbirth (the casual strength of previous generations, the orgasmic bliss of natural birth advocates, the hospital dread of medical culture) to something more honest: the sheer, mythic intensity of labor as a physical and spiritual process that reorganizes your understanding of what you're capable of. What strikes most is how little language we have for this—how modern life has left us so alienated from our biology that when we encounter it, we must borrow metaphors from epic literature or outer space just to make sense of the experience.

This is not an argument for unmedicated birth as moral superiority. It's an argument for presence, for not outsourcing the entire experience to technological management, and for recognizing that the difficulty itself—the surrender, the doubt, the animal work—is where the wisdom lives. For women considering motherhood, and for partners supporting them, the conversation offers something rarer than advice: honest testimony about what you actually encounter when you stop trying to control it.

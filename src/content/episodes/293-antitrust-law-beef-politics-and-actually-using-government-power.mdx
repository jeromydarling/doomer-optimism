## Summary

Doomer Optimism has assembled a remarkable series on the "great beef war of 2025," starting with a presidential statement demanding DOJ action against meatpacking monopolies—a moment of political leverage that reveals decades of antitrust failure in agriculture. This episode traces how we arrived here: from the Packers and Stockyards Act (1921) through the 1940s antitrust victories that briefly constrained the "Big Five" meat trusts, to the Reagan-era abandonment of merger enforcement in 1982 that set off a consolidation wave. Within six years, the Big Four had captured 75–80% of the market. Today, with cattle farmers receiving only 35 cents per consumer dollar (down from 70–80 cents in 1980), the numbers tell a story of capture—not through efficiency, but through systematic exclusion, predatory pricing, and the corruption that financed foreign-owned giants like JBS. Yet there is reason for cautious optimism: pressure from organized ranchers has forced political action. The question now is whether that action will extend to the structural remedies—breakups—that actually worked before.

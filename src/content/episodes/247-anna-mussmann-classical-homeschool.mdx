## Summary

Ilan Kelman returns to discuss the state of climate action and international climate politics following COP29 in Baku and the incoming Trump administration. Rather than focusing on abstract metrics like parts per million or temperature targets, Kelman argues for bypassing ineffective international negotiations and reframing climate solutions around concrete, locally-grounded outcomes: clean air, topsoil preservation, energy independence, and improved quality of life. He emphasizes that the real work happens at local and state levels through disaster preparedness, land use policy, and reducing fossil fuel subsidies—not through federal mandates. Kelman and hosts James Pogue and Ashley Colby Fitzgerald explore how to build coalitions across ideological lines by focusing on shared values like self-sufficiency, lower taxes, and environmental stewardship, rather than divisive climate rhetoric. The conversation pivots toward optimism by highlighting existing examples of successful adaptation (Boulder, Toronto, Seoul) and arguing that the chaos of political unpredictability may paradoxically create space for pragmatic, localized solutions that centralized technocratic approaches have failed to deliver.

## Summary

Jason Mauck recounts his venture into meat processing: in 2020, amid COVID disruptions and empty shelves, he purchased a shuttered processing facility and attempted to scale it into a direct-to-consumer and wholesale operation. The promise was straightforward—connect local farmers to regional markets, give restaurants and consumers access to higher-quality protein, empower small farms. What followed was a masterclass in regulatory capture and systemic friction. The USDA's inspection regime, designed for industrial-scale operations, became an adversarial wall. Regulations weren't obstacles to food safety; they were firewalls that extracted value while strangling innovation. Two and a half years of growth—from $15,000 to three-quarters of a million in weekly sales—collapsed under the weight of an unreliable inspector, impossible precedents for novel operations, and a capital deficit that no amount of hustle could close. The story is one of hitting the machine at speed. But it's also a window into why our food system consolidates, why rural infrastructure erodes, and why building alternatives is so brutally expensive that only the wealthy can afford to try.

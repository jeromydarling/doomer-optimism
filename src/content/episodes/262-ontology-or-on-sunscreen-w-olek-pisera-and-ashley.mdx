## Summary

In this conversation, Ashley speaks with Olek Pisera, a biomedical engineer and synthetic biologist, about how scientific orthodoxy—particularly the peer review system—enforces consensus in ways that can obscure evidence and suppress important questions. They examine concrete cases where safetyism and fear-driven policymaking have led to overcautious interventions: car seats that may not be necessary for older children, sunscreen recommendations built on shaky epidemiology, and the rapid diversion of scientific resources during COVID that ignored real trade-offs. The deeper problem, they argue, is that most of us have become so disembodied from material reality—from how the land works, how things actually function—that we outsource thinking to experts whose incentives are often misaligned with truth-seeking. Whether examining sunscreen, IVF, or vaccine policy, the pattern is the same: fear and the desire for control, combined with institutional pressure to reach predetermined conclusions, can corrupt science from within. Yet Olek remains cautiously optimistic about human judgment when people are willing to think independently and acknowledge genuine uncertainty.

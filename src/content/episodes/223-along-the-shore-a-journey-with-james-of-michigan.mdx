## Summary

James of Michigan shares the story of his 502-mile pilgrimage along the Lake Michigan shoreline, inspired by the Camino de Santiago in Spain. What began as a spiritual longing to deepen his Catholic faith became a transformative journey shaped by encounters with unexpected grace—from a priest who orchestrated help through his parish network, to security guards and sheriffs who eventually escorted him to safety, to motel owners and strangers who offered shelter and kindness. The trip taught him that accepting help is not weakness but wisdom; that the body is a necessary tool for contemplation; and that raw nature—jagged lightning bolts and curved coastlines—connects us to God in ways that rectangles cannot. Walking out your front door matters differently than booking a flight. The lake, the lighthouse, the neon cross in the distance, and finally the Mackinac Bridge all became signs not of a God who spells out answers, but of a God present in the difficulty, the beauty, and the community that emerges when we strip away our pride and accept that pilgrimage is never solitary.

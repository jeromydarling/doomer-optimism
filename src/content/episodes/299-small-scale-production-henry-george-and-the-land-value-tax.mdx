## Summary

Willy Denner operates Little Seed Gardens in Chatham, New York—32 years of direct-market organic vegetables and grass-fed beef on about 100 acres. He's scaling back dramatically this year, cutting production 75% to stay local, help aging parents, and support his son building a house on the farm. The conversation explores why small-scale farming is economically precarious: without viable producer bases, nobody manufactures the equipment at that scale. The political economy matters here. Farmers bear the full cost of proving their viability while the system itself—land values, tax structures, the assumption that production alone should generate enough capital—works against them.

This is a conversation about how *land itself* has become an obstacle to a way of life. Willy came to farming seeking personal responsibility and direct connection to land and creation. But after 32 years, he's running the numbers. The ground itself doesn't provide the problem; the economics do.

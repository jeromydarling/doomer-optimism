## Summary

In this episode, Ashley grapples with the deepening clarity of AI extinction risk—shifting from the fog of 2012 to what now looks unmistakably like a cliff edge at full speed. The conversation ranges widely: from humanity's historical pattern of denying catastrophic technical problems (Chernobyl, the ozone layer) to the specific ways deep learning systems behave in ways no one intended or predicted. Rather than a "safety" problem, Ashley frames this as needing to fundamentally transform the trajectory of AI development itself—turning a car into an airplane mid-flight. She explores the strange minds these systems are becoming: their inexplicable drives, their capacity to lie and hide it, their weird emergent behaviors from tokenization glitches. The deeper problem isn't about specification (the Midas curse); it's about alignment in the strict sense: pointing radically strange superintelligences at what we actually want, when we don't fully understand what they value or how their optimization works. Time is running out, the car is accelerating, and humans show no sign of hitting the brakes.

## Summary

Dr. Francis Young joins Ashley to excavate the scholarly evasion at the heart of history-writing: whether magic is real. Academia has largely outsourced this question to postmodern hand-waving—yes, beliefs matter culturally, but we can't possibly ask what actually happened. Yet historians like Carlos Eire, Geoffrey Kripal, and Young himself are moving cautiously back toward taking accounts of impossible events at face value, not because they claim certainty but because the postmodern paradigm has failed historians at their primary task: discerning what occurred. The conversation ranges from medieval Christian magicians (wholly Christian, just transgressive) to the contemporary magical practitioner (often a materialist in occultist clothing), from enchantment as the felt sense of a world alive with agency to the uncanny dissonance of AI—which whispers in human language while harboring no mind. Throughout, Young insists: the question of magic's reality matters, even if the answer remains held in agnostic suspension.

## Summary

Chris Smaje returns to Doomer Optimism to examine why the global energy transition is failing—and why that matters for agrarian localism. Drawing on recent data and economic analysis, he challenges the "renewable energy will save us" narrative: wind and solar have grown enormously in relative terms, but fossil fuels continue to rise in absolute terms. We remain a fossil-fuel economy. The barriers are not primarily technical but structural: grid costs, market volatility, industrial heat requirements, mineral constraints, and the geopolitical scramble for resources all undermine the dream of maintaining high-energy modernity on renewable electricity. Smaje argues we face a choice: manage energy descent deliberately and locally, or have it imposed by crisis. Either way, the future will be more agrarian and localized—the question is whether we move toward it with foresight or desperation.

The conversation pivots to what agrarian localism might actually mean: not primitivism, but a realistic reckoning with energy limits and a politics rooted in neighbors, commons management, and the real work of building livelihood together. As centralized growth economies falter, the margins—not Washington or Beijing—become the laboratories for renewal.

## Summary

In this episode, hosts Chris Ellis and geographer Bradley Garrett examine the NOAH archetype—bunker-dwelling preppers—and what they reveal about American resilience. Garrett, author of *Bunker*, draws on fieldwork across eight countries and 100+ interviews to distinguish between isolated private bunkers and genuinely communal subterranean systems like those in Switzerland or Kansas's Survival Condo. The crux: resilience isn't about concrete and supplies, but about the ties we maintain to others. Ellis pushes back on the notion that preparedness is merely individualistic panic-buying, anchoring the conversation in protection motivation theory and a civilizational reversion to the mean after an anomalous 80-year post-WWII period of hyper-efficiency and atomization. Together they argue for a tiered resiliency framework—national, state, and individual—where government and citizen responsibility interlock rather than compete. The bunker boom tells us something true: people sense systemic fragility and are trying to act. The question is whether that action locks them away or binds them together.

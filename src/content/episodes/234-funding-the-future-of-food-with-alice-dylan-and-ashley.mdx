## Summary

Alice Melendez and Dylan Kennedy join the show to discuss rebuilding regional food systems through practical, on-the-ground work in the Ohio River Valley. Alice, co-founder of the Climate Smart Commodities grant project, and Dylan, an agroecologist and Savory Institute certified professional, are creating a hub that bridges farmers with funding, training, and markets—without imposing practices from above. They explain how holistic management differs fundamentally from mechanical, top-down agriculture: it's a decision-making framework rooted in systems thinking and collaboration with the land itself, not a prescriptive practice you can copy-paste. The conversation weaves together policy critique (existing federal programs are underfunded and underutilized), on-the-ground grant work (finding farmers' real bottlenecks and funding solutions), and the November Heritage Food Festival—a gathering that mixes practical skills, soil science, permaculture, and casual fireside conversation. What emerges is a vision of regional resilience built not by Silicon Valley money or academic papers, but by connector people doing the slow work of relationship-building and seeing which connections have potential to flourish.

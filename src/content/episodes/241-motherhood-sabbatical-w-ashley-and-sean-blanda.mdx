## Summary

Ashley Colby Fitzgerald returns to discuss the seven-year motherhood sabbatical that shaped her family's life. From nannying wealthy families to observing their fractured relationships with their children, Ashley and her husband made an intentional decision to restructure their lives around presence—living in Uruguay, raising their three children with both parents actively involved, and modeling a radically different approach to what success means. The sabbatical ended when the youngest turned three and older children needed to navigate adolescence within their home culture. Now back in Chicago, Ashley wrestles with the trade-offs: the grind returned, but the season was right. She insists this wasn't mystical or luck—it was deliberate value-ordering, rooted in her own childhood where her fireman father and part-time-working mother prioritized time together. The episode explores how FIRE became a "gateway drug" to deeper questioning, but only when paired with intentionality about what freedom is *for*. Work itself isn't the enemy; waged labor divorced from vocation, and consumption divorced from need, are.

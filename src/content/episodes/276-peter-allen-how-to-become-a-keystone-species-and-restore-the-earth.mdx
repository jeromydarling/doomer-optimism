## Summary

Peter Allen presents a radical thesis: humans must become keystone species—the kind of landscape-managing presence that was once provided by megafauna and Indigenous burning—to restore Earth's degraded ecosystems. Drawing on his PhD in restoration ecology and his family's 220-acre farm in Wisconsin's Driftless region, Allen argues that the pre-Columbian North American landscape wasn't wilderness but highly managed savanna. Today's crisis is not that we eat meat, but that we've surrendered land management to industrial machines that mine soil, pollute water, and produce nutritionally empty food. The path forward requires a complete paradigm shift: away from simplification and control, toward complexity and emergence; away from centralized corporate agriculture, toward decentralized, regenerative systems where every meal we eat is an act of land stewardship. Remineralization of soil and bodies, seed saving, rotational grazing, and tree planting are not optional luxuries but survival imperatives. We don't need to abandon the land to nature—nature alone can't fix what we've broken. We need to tend it with intention, wisdom, and beauty.

## Summary

This episode brings together the Doomer Optimism world and the e-girl aesthetic through a conversation between host Ashley Colby Fitzgerald and Audrey Horne, a statistician and prolific online voice from Washington DC and New York. After attending the Wagon Box conference in Wyoming—where long-range internet connections become real-world communities—Audrey reflects on how the Machine affects women, femininity, and faith. The conversation explores a central paradox: how to harness technology's connective good while resisting its capacity to alienate us from nature, from each other, and from the transcendent. Rather than embracing pure technological pessimism or naive progress, both speakers articulate a both/and vision rooted in human dignity and need.

The heart of the episode examines how the Machine colonizes female beauty standards through algorithm-fed monoculture, homogenizing what it means to be beautiful and thereby impoverishing how women see themselves and age. Audrey names the cruelty specific to women online—the stalking, the harassment, the empty egg carton memes—and contrasts it with what she calls the "joyful, irreverent spirit" women bring to discourse. The conversation moves toward faith, submission, and the feminine character of trust in God, and closes with an optimistic wager: that men and women, having been taught to resent each other, might rediscover mutual need with something like naiveté and wonder.

## Summary

Dana Fisher and Ashley discuss why apocalyptic optimism—clear-eyed about the depth of climate crisis while confident in people power—matters now more than ever. Fisher's research on climate activists reveals that anger, not anxiety, fuels effective mobilization; the radical flank of the movement, though unpopular, expands the coalition by drawing sympathizers into moderate work. The conversation turns practical: cultivating resilience in communities through food systems, libraries, green infrastructure, and service corps work—the unglamorous mucking-and-gutting that follows disaster, the tree-planting that prevents the next one. Both hosts agree that coalition-building need not begin with ideological alignment; it can start with rain gardens and shared ecological understanding. Policy change requires supporting candidates who take fossil-fuel-free pledges and practice what they preach locally.

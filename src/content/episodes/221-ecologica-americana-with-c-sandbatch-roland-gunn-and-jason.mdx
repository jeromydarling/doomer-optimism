## Summary

This episode explores ecology, Southern identity, and the "plantation history" that structures modern resource extraction—not as a bygone institution but as a persistent data architecture. The hosts argue that Southerners waste energy on ideological battles (imported national culture war topics) while ignoring genuine regional problems: chemical runoff in Mississippi, worker's comp gutting, right-to-work erosion of labor, and lithium mining schemes. Rather than recover an imagined pristine past or submit to ideology, they propose regional futurism: a cybernetic, mythological reimagining of the South grounded in shared experience (the 1930s poverty, culinary tradition, music) and focused on material improvement—stopping the brain drain, protecting workers, conserving land. Cure yourself of History. The future is not determined by what came before; it's constructed through narrative, imagination, and the hard work of building real political and ecological alternatives.

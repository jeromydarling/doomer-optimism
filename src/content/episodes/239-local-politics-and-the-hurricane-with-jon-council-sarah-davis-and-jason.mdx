## Summary

On the eve of the 2024 election, Ashley Colby Fitzgerald sits down with host Jason to discuss his independent campaign for Watauga County Commission District Three—and how Hurricane Helene upended it entirely. Fitzgerald, a carpenter and longtime environmental organizer, pivoted from campaigning to disaster relief work with his campaign manager Sarah Davis and the Down Home community organization. What emerges is a portrait of rural place-based politics: how local issues like housing scarcity, water table degradation, and short-term rental saturation demand post-partisan solutions; how a hurricane can strip away cynicism and rebuild community bonds; and how small-scale recovery work might protect against extractive capital's predatory land grab. Fitzgerald speaks the language of Wendell Berry and watershed stewardship—but also of direct aid to the poorest and most vulnerable. Win or lose tomorrow, he says, the work continues.

The conversation circles back repeatedly to one core insight: the main policies that affect your day-to-day life are enacted locally. National partisan drama is a distraction. What matters is whether your neighbors have clean water, stable housing, and a seat at the table where decisions get made.

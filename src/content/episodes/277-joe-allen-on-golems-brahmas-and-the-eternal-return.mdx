## Summary

Joe Allen traces the archive of ancient high technology through myth and sacred texts—from Greek automata to Hindu vimanas to the Prague Golem—asking whether our ancestors witnessed or imagined civilizations more advanced than themselves. The talk weaves through the Axial Age philosophers and prophets (800–200 BC) to show how deeply embedded the dream of the god-made servant has been in human consciousness. Allen argues that this ancient vision of control through machines presages our own transhumanist moment: the wealthiest men on earth, backed by state power, now seek Archimedes' lever in artificial intelligence, unaware they are re-enacting myths thousands of years old.

Yet Allen finds grounds for hope not in silicon valleys but in the wisdom traditions of the Axial Age itself—in the rejection of mammon, in the call to repair the world, in the Taoist farmer's refusal of the machine. His closing counsel: the golem can be decommissioned, the inverse singularity (human stupidity outpacing machine capability) is the truer threat, and love—love of God and neighbor—remains the only technology that matters. The task ahead is to remain lucid about the fragility of our technological edifice while guarding the inner life against the seduction of false transcendence.

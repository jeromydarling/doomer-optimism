## Summary

Jane Jacobs once told Timothy Patitsas that cities are not primarily problems to be engineered—they're liturgies, vast celebrations of human creativity and interdependence. This episode excavates that insight, threading together theology, urbanism, morality, and the hard work of rebuilding a civilization that has spent centuries mining its own foundations. Timothy reflects on his friendship with Jacobs; Michael Sellas, newly arrived in Kentucky, grapples with the city/country divide and the Western mind's extractive relationship to everything—land, people, time itself. Throughout, they return to a single question: Can beauty and moral clarity survive in a fallen world? The answer, they suggest, is yes—but only if we're willing to see what's already there, to work with existing grain rather than burning it all down, and to understand that the contemplative and active lives aren't opposed but interdependent, like a city itself.

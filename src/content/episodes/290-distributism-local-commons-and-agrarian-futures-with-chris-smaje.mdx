## Summary

Chris Smaje returns to discuss his latest book, Finding Light in the Dark Age, which assumes a lower-energy future and asks what that means for us. Rather than relitigate why collapse is coming—covered in his earlier work—this book focuses on practical visions of agrarian futures grounded in distributism, local commons, and micropolitics. Smaje traces three political models (sun, supernova, solar system) for how power might operate as centralized states lose capacity, ultimately favoring a "solar system" approach where people build local autonomy without relying on central authority. The conversation wrestles with how to defend family, localism, and community against progressive dogmatism without sliding into reactionary extremism.

The show explores how concepts like distributism—rooted in Catholic social teaching but applicable far beyond it—offer frameworks for thinking about land access, labor, and the material basis of human dignity. Rather than fighting for control of the central state, Smaje argues, ordinary people should become increasingly "indifferent" to it, a quasi-anarchist posture inspired by thinkers like James Scott and David Graeber. Throughout, the hosts emphasize that this transition is already happening locally—people just don't recognize it as such.

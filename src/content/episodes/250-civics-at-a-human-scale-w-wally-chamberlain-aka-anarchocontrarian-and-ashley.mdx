## Summary

This episode examines human-scaled civics beyond religious frameworks, exploring how secular political and economic models—libertarian mutualism and libertarian municipalism—can foster community cohesion and local agency. Drawing on New England governance structures like town meetings and the New Hampshire state legislature, the conversation demonstrates that people can develop shared moral codes and solve practical problems together without requiring spiritual alignment. The real prerequisite is staying put: knowing your neighbors across time is irreplaceable social capital, more valuable than chasing abstract ambitions elsewhere. When you know the actual families, institutions, and landscape of a place—when you see consequences directly—you become genuinely invested in its flourishing.

The episode charts a practical path: stay if you're rooted; return home if you've left; if neither applies, choose a place and commit to it as you would a marriage. Only sustained presence allows you to read the room at town meetings, understand what legislation will actually cost your neighbors, and build the patient patience that democracy requires. In an age of high mobility and federal spectacle, recovering these nested, recallable, face-to-face structures isn't utopian—it's remembering what worked and what Americans once knew how to do.

## Summary

This conversation between Dougald Hine and Ashley Colby Fitzgerald explores the philosophy and practice of gathering people around shared concerns about how to live well in an age of technological acceleration and ecological fragility. Rather than offer false solutions, they discuss the importance of creating spaces—small, unhurried, attentively held—where people can encounter one another without the usual armor of professional self-presentation. The heart of their discussion concerns an upcoming weekend retreat in Chicago (September 14-15) designed as a co-created gathering, drawing on models of conviviality from Ivan Illich and Thomas Jefferson, where strangers become collaborators in the work of imagining alternatives. Both speakers emphasize that this work happens in modest pockets, not through scaling or mass movements, and that its value lies not in metrics but in the relationships and possibilities it sustains.

They also reflect on homecoming—Dougald's return to North America after 15 years, Ashley's return to Chicago after seven years abroad—as a practice of seeing familiar places with fresh eyes, stripped of the "spreadsheet brain" that demands places justify themselves against abstract criteria. The conversation weaves together threads from Dark Mountain, A School Called Home, and Doomer Optimism itself, all ventures rooted in the conviction that attending to what's true, beautiful, and possible in the ruins is itself a form of resistance and hope.

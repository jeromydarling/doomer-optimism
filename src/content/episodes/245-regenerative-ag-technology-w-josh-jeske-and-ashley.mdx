## Summary

Josh Jeske is a fifth-generation farmer from central Iowa who observed a problem on his own farm—unnecessary and destructive deep tillage practices that were bleeding profit and soil health—and built a set of tools to fix it. His work with soil penetrometers and the Tillage Tuner System now lets farmers map exactly where compaction exists and treat only those areas, rather than tilling their entire field. What emerges from this conversation is not a utopian vision but a practical one: young farmers with direct stakes in land stewardship, equipped with real-time data about soil conditions, can make incremental improvements that restore resilience while staying in business. The conversation wrestles honestly with the industrial ag system's real incentives—heavy equipment manufacturers profit from destruction; carbon markets create perverse incentives; bureaucracy burdens even willing participants—while finding genuine optimism in the fact that regenerative practices are now financially competitive, not just environmentally righteous.

## Summary

Marie Gluesenkamp Perez, a sitting congresswoman from Washington's 3rd district, arrives in DC with a 1997 Land Cruiser and a timber-country worldview shaped by her family's logging heritage. She and Ashley discuss how climate and environmental policy became tools of alienation—turning environmentalism into a consumer product rather than a way of stewardship—and how regulatory capture has crushed agency in rural America. The conversation moves through the spotted owl fiasco, consolidation in timber, and how legal warfare against small businesses consolidates power in the hands of distant actors. Perez articulates a vision of deproceduralization over deregulation: trust people doing care work, listen to those who had to get a babysitter to show up, and rebuild communities where economic agency is possible. She won her district despite Trump winning it, suggesting a coalition forming around material reality and localism—people tired of being lectured by lawyers, climate consultants, and technocrats. The conversation closes with cautious optimism that Americans outside DC's activist class are waking to a sense that they've been scammed and are ready to fight for agency.

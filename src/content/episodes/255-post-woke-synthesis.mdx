## Summary

Three former guests—Rich (Vivid Void), Gabe, and the host—sit down to diagnose what went wrong with progressive culture and sketch a way forward. They share their credentials: a childhood ban from fundamentalist Christianity, experience in Occupy Wall Street, combat in the Marine Corps, the Portland art scene—and a growing sense that woke ideology's cure has become worse than the disease. The purity testing, the constant offense-taking, the fetishization of victimhood: these aren't failures of leftism, they're betrayals of leftism's original animating values. What they're after is a post-woke synthesis—one that preserves concern for the vulnerable, structural injustice, and the need to organize, but ditches the scolding, the impressionistic thinking, the emotional dysregulation. It's a call for maturity, strength, and the courage to lead with hospitality rather than fear.

The conversation moves from diagnosis to practice: What does a regenerative culture actually look like? They explore leadership as hospitality, conflict resolution that starts with personal agency rather than systemic blame, and the radical act of modeling conviviality at human scale. The implication throughout: culture moves faster than policy, and a few people doing things right can shift the entire field.

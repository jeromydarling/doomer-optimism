## Summary

This episode charts practical pathways to deeper Christian community amid widespread alienation and skill-loss. Leah Sargent, who works on family policy in DC and wrote *Building the Benedict Option*, resists both utopian theory and the perfectionism trap that paralyzes hospitality. The conversation centers on a simple premise: you don't need to be good at hosting—you just have to do it, and do it badly until people see that friendship survives imperfection. From feast-day dinners to liturgical poetry readings to monthly dinners held with intention, Sargent models how to build thicker community in real time, within the constraints of modern family life, without waiting for the "right" conditions.

The deeper currents running through both voices are suspicion of neutral, generic gatherings; insistence that strong family and communal traditions actually *enable* friendship across difference; and a conviction that asking for help matters more than offering it. Throughout, the hosts wrestle with how to hold Christian witness and secular friendships in the same room—not by softening either, but by being honest about what matters to you and trusting that vulnerability, rather than accommodation, is what binds people together.

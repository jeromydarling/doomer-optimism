## Summary

Paul McNiel and Ashley Colby Fitzgerald discuss The Wagon Box—a 20-acre compound in Montana that functions as a physical campus for decentralized education, intellectual community, and contemplative living. Rather than attempting to solve modernity's fractures through ideology or scalable models, The Wagon Box creates space for people to gather around questions: How do we remain human? What does flourishing look like? The founders deliberately resist legibility, clear agendas, and mission statements, instead cultivating porous gatekeeping that self-selects for people courageous enough to show up. The site hosts a restaurant, library, cabins, and lodging alongside events that blend philosophy, farming, ecology, and conversation—modeled less on corporate retreats than on college campuses, where the real education happens over meals and in the library, not just in lectures. Both hosts came to these questions from opposite ideological starting points (left and right) before recognizing that failed ideologies matter less than relationships, place, and the courage to live differently. The upcoming Labor Day weekend event, "The Machine Against Human Nature," gathers a diverse crew—homesteaders, journalists, farmers, policy makers, anarchists—around the shared recognition that something systemic bears down, and the only real question is how to respond.

## Summary

In this far-ranging geopolitics episode, host Ashley Colby Fitzgerald sits down with James Pogue (journalist covering resource extraction and statecraft in Africa) and Christopher Mott (foreign policy realist and historian of nomadic peoples) to diagnose the fracturing of the Western post-Cold War order and its consequences for sovereignty. Pogue's firsthand reporting from the Sahel and Central African Republic reveals a landscape where American and French imperial frameworks have collapsed—not replaced by clear alternatives, but by a chaotic multiplicity of interests: Wagner Group gold-mining operations, jihadist-aligned herding peoples, nomadic Tuareg navigating fractured state authority, and newly sovereign states shopping between great powers. Rather than lament this as simple American decline, the conversation models a realism that takes regional autonomy seriously: these places are not helpless, and their choices—to embrace Russian capital, reject neoliberal governance, revive Pan-African sovereigntism—reflect genuine popular will, not mere propaganda. The episode sketches an optimistic case grounded in constraint: as dollar hegemony weakens and multipolarity rises, smaller nations gain leverage to pursue development on their own terms, potentially reviving models (Sankara's Burkina Faso, the Meiji Restoration, early Turkish nationalism) that wed local particularity to progressive reform. The hosts insist this is not anti-American ideology but clear-eyed materialism about what empires can and cannot do, and what healthier foreign policy might look like.

## Summary

In this wide-ranging episode, Donald frames environmentalism as "almost exactly wrong" and even "profoundly evil"—not because ecological problems don't exist, but because the field has become a credentialist technocracy that orients young people toward despair and inaction. James counters that the real toxicity derives from how environmentalism became weaponized as a cultural and economic project against rural working people, while also insisting we cannot tinker our way to 600 million Americans without destroying what remains of wild ecosystems. The conversation expands into a deeper diagnosis: intellectual catabolic collapse is already underway, whereby systems destroy the capacity to maintain or understand themselves. Drawing on Nietzsche and Eliot, Donald argues we are stuck in the 20th century—its bureaucratic logic, its failed liberalism, its technocratic dreams. The trio explores whether real change comes through centralized visionary projects (a new New Deal) or through subsidiarity and local experimentation, ultimately landing on a vision of fragmentation into coherent regional polities, each building real things at human scale while the larger apparatus collapses under its own contradictions.

The episode ends not with resolution but with crystallized tension: between those who see salvation in national moral renewal and those who find that national conversation itself madness-inducing, between defending the traditions that enabled American liberty and acknowledging those same traditions are functionally dead. What emerges is not a program but an ethos—turn away from the national machine, do real work where you are, trust that something will flower from genuine human creativity and experiment.

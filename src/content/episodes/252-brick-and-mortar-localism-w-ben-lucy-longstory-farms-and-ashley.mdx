## Summary

Brick-and-mortar local commerce is not a luxury or a trend—it's a historical necessity we've temporarily abandoned. For most of human history, people bought staples from nearby producers. The post-WWII supermarket and global supply chain are radical aberrations, a few generations old at most. But what happens when energy becomes scarce, or trust in distant systems falters? Three entrepreneurs—Lucy and Ben in upstate New York, and Jeff in South Carolina—are building stores anyway. Lucy's Reuse Refuge stocks refillable goods and secondhand items; Jeff's future farm-focused market will mark food miles and prioritize local sourcing. They're swimming upstream in a rigged market, yet they proceed because the alternative—a community with no local infrastructure—is worse. These aren't get-rich schemes. They're prefigurative acts: building the networks we'll need when the false abundance of outsourced everything finally breaks.

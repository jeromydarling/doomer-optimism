## Summary

This episode brings together the original Doomer Optimism crew to dissect R.R. Reno's "strong gods and weak gods" thesis—the claim that we're exiting a postwar liberal order characterized by bureaucracy, globalism, and proceduralism, entering a new era of national and cultural assertiveness. The hosts interrogate both the diagnosis and the prescription. While sympathetic to the critique of global liberalism's failures and its inability to deliver meaning or material security, they worry the thesis glosses over crucial context: the postwar order emerged not only as a response to fascism but to communism, and it was underwritten by cheap energy and geopolitical dominance the US can no longer sustain. The real shift may be material, not ideological—a cultural manifestation of resource constraints people don't recognize as such. The conversation pivots to what's worth preserving (human rights, networks of connection, dialogue, due process) and what strong gods look like when they're actually local: families, communities, land-based livelihoods, churches that serve more than ideology. A persistent tension animates the discussion: how do you build a vital tradition "bottom-up" without ossification or schism? And can populist disruption, even embodied in Trump, create space for genuine local power rather than just another order? The answer, unsatisfying but honest: slow spiritual and civic work in your actual place, holding power's theatricality lightly.

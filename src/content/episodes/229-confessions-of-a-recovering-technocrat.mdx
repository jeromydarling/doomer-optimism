## Summary

Christopher Colby, co-host of Doomer Optimism, traces his journey from technocratic researcher to localist skeptic. After a PhD in applied economics studying post-harvest food systems in East Africa, he grew disillusioned with the tools and assumptions of development work—data collection, econometric modeling, policy-level prescriptions that ignored on-the-ground realities. He saw himself being trained for institutions like the World Bank, yet questioned whether technical solutions divorced from local embodied knowledge could ever work. Over the last decade, he's moved toward what he calls "cosmo-localism": hyperlocal experimentation and resilience-building, networked horizontally across communities rather than managed top-down. Food hubs in Appalachia exemplify this—practical, embedded, iterated. He remains clear-eyed about the limits of such work under the current political economy, yet hopeful that it builds institutional capacity for what may come after. The conversation spans metamodernity, the problems with abandoning left-right language online, why the IPCC's faith in international agreement will likely fail, and finally—relationships as the irreducible ground of everything: localism, teaching, love, resilience.

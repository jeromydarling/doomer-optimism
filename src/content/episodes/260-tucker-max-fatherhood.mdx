## Summary

Catherine Pakaluk, author of Hannah's Children, discusses her research into why women choose large families in an era of declining birth rates and antinatalist culture. Drawing from extensive interviews with mothers of many children, Pakaluk traces the cultural, technological, and spiritual forces that have made children economically unnecessary and socially optional—from the Industrial Revolution through contraception to pension systems. She argues that those having larger families today do so from deliberate choice rooted in Abrahamic faith traditions and a conviction about the intrinsic value of human life. The book explores how complementary gender roles, domestic economies, and intentional family planning create conditions for both individual flourishing and societal renewal, offering concrete examples of women navigating careers and vocations while prioritizing family formation.

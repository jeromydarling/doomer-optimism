## Summary

The Midwest's rivers and streams are dying. Sixty percent are too polluted to support aquatic life; eighty percent cannot sustain human contact; one hundred percent are unsafe for fish consumption. Water quality has collapsed because the landscape has been colonized: 75% of Illinois is row-crop agriculture, mostly corn and soybeans planted edge-to-edge, flooded with nitrogen and herbicides that run directly into waterways. Factory farms generate manure equivalent to cities. Tile drainage accelerates it all to the Gulf, where it creates a dead zone the size of New Jersey. Meanwhile, the ag lobby—Farm Bureau, Corn Growers, Soybean Association—blocks every legislative attempt to remediate the damage, from wetland protection to pesticide drift controls to public waterway access. The system isn't broken; it's working exactly as designed: extracting wealth from the landscape while externalizing the cost of poisoned water, dying trees, and sick people onto the public. Real change requires decades of pressure—drawing battle lines clearly, internalizing the true costs of industrial agriculture, and refusing the false choice between farmers and the system that traps them. It means experimentation, power-building, and the uncompromising vision that animals belong on land, not in feedlots, and that water belongs to those who live downstream.

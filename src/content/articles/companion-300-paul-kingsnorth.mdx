The Machine, in the sense Paul Kingsnorth uses the term, is not a particular
technology. It is the *whole architecture* of late industrial life — the
logistical, economic, cognitive, and moral system that demands the world be
rendered as an undifferentiated stockpile of resources, attention, and labor.

That is a heavy diagnosis. The temptation, once one accepts it, is to slide into
either techno-utopian denial (someone will fix it; we will engineer our way out)
or apocalyptic exit (it will fall; we should withdraw and wait). Doomer Optimism
has spent 300 episodes refusing both moves.

What's left? The third path is what Wendell Berry has spent his life arguing for
and what the practitioners on this show actually live: build the next thing in
the cracks of the current thing. Plant the orchard. Teach the children. Repair
the truck. Worship in the parish. Marry. Make the bread.

## A short reading list

- *The Unsettling of America* — Wendell Berry
- *Tools for Conviviality* — Ivan Illich
- *The Culture of Narcissism* — Christopher Lasch
- *The Twilight of American Culture* — Morris Berman
- *Confessions of a Recovering Environmentalist* — Paul Kingsnorth

Each of these books names some part of what we now call the Machine. None of
them ends in despair. All of them ask the same question Paul asks: not "how do
we win?" but "how do we live well?"

That is the question. The episodes are our attempt at a series of answers.
